**No - dogs are not allowed on any public beach in Okaloosa County, which covers Destin, Okaloosa Island, and Fort Walton Beach, and the City of Destin bans animals on its beaches too; in Walton County (30A, Miramar Beach, Santa Rosa Beach) a leashed dog is allowed only from 3:30 PM to 8:30 AM with an annual county permit that only residents and property owners can get; Navarre Beach, the state parks, and the National Seashore are off limits; so the nearest Gulf sand where a visiting dog is legal is the designated dog beaches on Pensacola Beach and the dog beach west of the City Pier in Panama City Beach.**

If you are here for a month with a Lab, the first question is usually the same: which beach can we take him to? The honest answer is one nobody likes. Not this one. Not that one either. Probably not the one the rental listing said was dog friendly.

I was born and raised in Destin. I have watched a lot of well-meaning people walk a dog down a beach access at sunset, get halfway to the water, and meet a deputy. It is not a scam and it is not a rule the locals ignore. It is the code, it has been the code for a long time, and it covers more sand than most visitors expect.

So here is the whole coast in one place, county by county, with the ordinance behind each rule. I checked every rule below against the current county and city code, not against a travel blog. Rules change, and a posted sign always governs. If you want the short version for a specific spot, the [Dog Beach Checker](/tools/dog-beach-checker/) sorts it by location and tells you whether you are a resident or a visitor for the answer.

## Okaloosa County: no dogs on public beaches

This is the one that surprises people most, because Destin feels like a dog town. Plenty of patios welcome them. The beach does not.

Okaloosa County's code says no dog or cat is permitted on the public beaches of the county unless the county has posted a sign authorizing it. That is Section 5-25 of the county code, and I have not found a single spot where the county has posted such a sign. The rule applies to residents and visitors the same way, and it applies to leashed dogs. A leash does not make the sand legal. A violation is a civil infraction with a fine.

That covers Okaloosa Island, the beaches behind The Boardwalk, and the public Gulf beaches around Fort Walton Beach. The original covenants for Okaloosa Island say the same thing in plainer language: pets are not permitted on the beaches at all.

## The City of Destin: a second ban on top of the first

Destin has its own ordinance, separate from the county's. Section 4-7 of the city code says no animal is permitted on the public beaches of the city unless a city sign authorizes it. It says "animal," not "dog," so it covers everything. The city's own FAQ puts it in capital letters: dogs are not allowed on the beaches in Destin or Okaloosa County.

The city points dog owners to the Nancy Weidenhamer Dog Park on Indian Bayou Trail, open sunrise to sunset. Your dog stays leashed until it is inside the fence.

Two places people ask about constantly are the Destin Harbor Boardwalk and Crab Island. I could not find a specific official rule on dogs for either one. The harbor boardwalk is not a beach, so normal leash rules apply there. Crab Island is a sandbar in the pass you reach by boat, and I am not going to tell you it is fine when nobody official has said so.

## State parks and the National Seashore: trails yes, sand no

Florida's state park rule bars pets from beaches and swimming areas, both the land and the water side, and caps leashes at six feet everywhere else. Each park near here spells it out:

- **Henderson Beach State Park** in Destin: no pets on the beach. Leashed dogs are allowed in the parking areas, on the nature trail, and on the day-use sidewalk.
- **Grayton Beach State Park**: no pets on beaches.
- **Topsail Hill Preserve State Park**: no pets on the beach, the tram, the boardwalks, or the dunes. Leashed dogs can use the trails.
- **Deer Lake State Park**: pets are allowed in the park, but not on the boardwalk or the beach.

Gulf Islands National Seashore, which includes the Okaloosa Area on the island and Opal Beach out past Navarre, bans pets on all park beaches on both the Gulf and bay side. Leashed pets are allowed on trails, paths, and roads.

A Walton County dog permit does not change any of this. State and federal parks run their own rules.

## Walton County: permit holders only, 3:30 PM to 8:30 AM

Walton is the county that allows dogs on the beach, and it is also where the most wrong information lives. You will find blogs quoting four different sets of hours. The ordinance settles it.

Walton County's beach code, Section 22-31, as amended in November 2025, bans animals on public beaches with two exceptions: service animals, and leashed dogs with a county dog beach permit. The permit allows leashed dogs on the beach from 3:30 PM to 8:30 AM the next morning. It is year-round. There is no summer schedule and no winter schedule.

The catch is who can get one. The county issues permits only to Walton County real property owners and permanent residents. If you are renting a house on 30A for a month, you are not eligible, and neither is your dog. If you own a place in South Walton and winter here, you are.

The rest of the conditions are what you would expect. The dog stays on a leash under your direct control. You pick up after it. You need proof of rabies vaccination to apply. The tag goes on the collar or in your pocket, it is not transferable, and every permit expires at the end of July, so a permit from last winter does not cover this one. Permits come from the county's Code Compliance office in Santa Rosa Beach. I am not listing the fee here, since the county sets it and can change it. The county's dog beach page has the current amount.

## Navarre Beach: no

Santa Rosa County's code makes it unlawful to let an animal on public beaches, school grounds, or playgrounds, except in designated dog-friendly parks. The county's Navarre Beach page is blunter: no pets allowed on the beach. There is no official dog beach on Navarre Beach. The nearest county option is Navarre Central Bark, a dog park on High School Boulevard, open dawn to dusk.

## Where a visiting dog can legally go

This is the part people actually need.

**Pensacola Beach dog beaches.** Escambia County runs two designated dog beaches on Pensacola Beach, one on the west side next to Parking Lot B and one on the east side next to Parking Lot E. The Santa Rosa Island Authority's website labels the same spots with its own lot numbers, so do not let the two naming systems throw you. From November through April they are open sunrise to sunset. From May through October, 7 AM to sunset. Dogs must be on a leash no longer than eight feet, held at all times, and wear their license tags. Every other stretch of Pensacola Beach is off limits.

**Perdido Key Dog Park.** Escambia also has a dog park on River Road on Perdido Key, open sunrise to sunset, where dogs can run loose inside the park.

**Panama City Beach.** The city's code bans animals on the Gulf sand, and the city designates one exception: the beach on the west side of the City Pier is dog friendly, with dogs on a leash. It is about an hour east of Destin, and it is the one people mean when they say "the dog beach at Pier Park."

**Fenced dog parks close to home.** Liza Jackson Park in Fort Walton Beach has a fenced dog park where dogs are allowed off leash. The park is in the middle of a renovation that started in September 2026, and the city says the dog parks stay open through it. In Destin, the Nancy Weidenhamer Dog Park. In Navarre, Central Bark. I have written about [why the dog park is not always the workout people think it is](/blog/dog-park-not-tiring-dog-out/), but for letting a dog stretch its legs off leash, they are the legal option.

## The beach was never the workout anyway

Here is the part where I talk you out of feeling cheated.

A beach day feels like the ultimate dog outing. In practice it is mostly standing around, a few sprints after a ball, a lot of salt water swallowed, and sand in places sand should not be. Soft sand is hard on a dog that is not used to it. Cornell's veterinary college lists sand alongside ice and slick floors as a surface where running dogs slip and get hurt. And on this coast, the Gulf side comes with its own problems in some seasons, like the ones I covered in [the red tide post](/blog/red-tide-dogs-emerald-coast/).

## FAQ

**Are dogs allowed on the beach in Destin?** No. Okaloosa County bans dogs and cats on all public beaches, and the City of Destin bans all animals on city beaches, unless a posted sign allows it. Leashed dogs are included, and the rules apply to residents and visitors alike.

**What are the dog beach hours in Walton County?** From 3:30 PM to 8:30 AM the next morning, year-round, on a leash, and only with a Walton County dog beach permit. The permit is available only to county property owners and permanent residents.

**Can visitors take their dog to the beach on 30A?** Not on the public beaches. The Walton County permit is limited to residents and property owners, so short-term renters and visitors are not eligible, and the state parks along 30A do not allow dogs on the beach.

**Is there a dog beach near Navarre?** Not on Navarre Beach. Santa Rosa County does not allow pets on its beaches. The nearest designated dog beaches are on Pensacola Beach, and the county's Navarre Central Bark dog park is the closest off-leash option.

**Where is the closest dog-friendly beach to Destin?** For a visitor, the designated dog beaches on Pensacola Beach to the west, or the dog beach west of the City Pier in Panama City Beach to the east. Walton County's beaches are closer, but only for permit holders during permit hours.

## The short version

On most of this coast, the sand is off limits to your dog, and the rules have been on the books for decades. Know the few places where it is legal, go at the right hours, and give the dog its real work somewhere that is not a beach.
