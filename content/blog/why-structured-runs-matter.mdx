Random yard time and weekend fetch are fine — they are not a conditioning plan. Dogs that carry serious drive often look “fine” on the outside while still carrying stress on the inside.

Structured runs give that drive a job. Pace is chosen on purpose, duration is bounded, and recovery is part of the session — not an accident when the dog finally quits.

## What owners notice first

Most families report the same pattern: the dog settles faster in the evening, sleeps deeper, and shows less frantic behavior at triggers like the doorbell or the leash rack.

That is not magic. It is output management. When the body gets a full workload, the nervous system has less leftover noise to spend on micro-meltdowns.

## How we think about safety

Heat, hydration, and gait all matter on the mill. We cap intensity to what the dog can hold with good form, and we stop for resets when we need to. You get honesty, not ego — if today is not the day for a hard push, we adapt.

## Putting it together

If you want a dog that is easier to live with without medicating the personality out of them, start with one intro session. You will feel the difference before we pack the truck.
