"Just take them for a longer walk" is the advice everyone gives. For a lot of dogs it does not work, and owners end up walking further and further while the dog comes home barely winded.

The problem is not effort. It is the type of work. A walk and a run do different things to a dog's body and brain, and if you only ever do one, you leave the other gap wide open.

## What a walk actually does

A good walk is mostly mental. Sniffing is decompression — it is how dogs read the world and lower their arousal. The pace is slow, the route is stop-start, and the heart rate rarely climbs and holds for long.

That is valuable. It is also why a walk rarely drains a high-drive dog. You can add miles and still send home a dog that is mentally satisfied and physically wide awake.

## What sustained running does

Conditioning is the opposite. The work is continuous, the heart rate climbs and stays up, and the body burns through real output instead of idling between sniffs.

That sustained effort is what high-drive dogs were built for and almost never get in daily life. It is also what produces the settled, deep-sleeping dog that owners are actually chasing when they keep extending the walk.

## The stop-start problem

Here is the part most people miss: a walk is paced by its slowest moment. Every sniff, every greeting, every stop at a corner resets the effort. The dog never gets into a rhythm.

A slatmill is self-powered — no motor, your dog sets the pace — but the surface keeps them honest. There is nothing to stop and investigate, so the effort stays continuous. Thirty focused minutes can do what two distracted miles cannot.

## Weather, time, and the Florida factor

On the Emerald Coast, the walk plan falls apart half the year. By mid-morning in summer the pavement is too hot and the humidity turns a walk into a slog for both of you. Conditioning that happens out of the heat does not care what the asphalt is doing at noon.

There is also the time math. Most people do not have two hours a day to walk a working dog into the ground. A structured session is built to get the job done in a window you can actually fit into a real schedule.

## Both have a place

This is not anti-walk. Keep the walks — your dog needs the sniffing and the decompression. Think of it as two jobs: the walk handles the mind, the run handles the body. Most high-drive dogs are getting one and missing the other.
