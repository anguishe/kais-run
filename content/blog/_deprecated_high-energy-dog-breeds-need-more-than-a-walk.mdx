Some dogs were designed to work all day. We bred them that way on purpose — to herd, to guard, to hunt, to pull — and then most of us moved them into houses with a backyard and a fifteen-minute walk. The drive did not go anywhere.

If you own one of these breeds, you already know a walk is not enough. Here is the short list of dogs that need real conditioning, not just activity.

## Belgian Malinois

The dog militaries and police choose for a reason. Relentless work ethic, endless engine. A bored Malinois will find a job, and you will not like the one they pick.

## German Shepherd

Built to work a full shift on their feet. Sharp, driven, and prone to anxiety and destructive habits when the output is not there.

## Border Collie

The smartest breed most people own, and the most easily under-stimulated. Work the body and the brain, or they invent problems to solve — usually involving your furniture.

## Siberian Husky

Bred to pull sleds across distances most dogs will never see. A husky that is not properly worked is the husky in every "rehome" post — vocal, escape-artist, impossible to tire with a stroll.

## Vizsla and Weimaraner

The velcro hunting breeds. Athletic, anxious when under-exercised, and deeply attached — which means their unspent energy lands directly on you.

## Rhodesian Ridgeback

Bred to cover ground and hold their own against big game. Powerful, independent, and far more dog than a casual walk can satisfy. This is the breed that started all of this — [Kai](/about/) is a Ridgeback mix, and his energy is the reason Kai's Run exists. Walks did not touch it. The slatmill did.

## Why the neighborhood loop does not touch them

These breeds share one trait: they were built for sustained output, not short bursts. A stop-start walk lets them idle. They come home physically unbothered and mentally still hunting for a job — which is when the pacing, the chewing, and the fence-clearing start.

More walking is not the fix. The fix is the right kind of work.

## What actually drains a working dog

Sustained, structured effort — heart rate up and holding, with a real warm-up and cool-down. That is what these dogs were bred to do, and it is exactly what daily life rarely provides. Get the output right and the "behavior problems" usually fade on their own, because they were never behavior problems. They were unmet needs.
