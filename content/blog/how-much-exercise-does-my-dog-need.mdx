How much exercise does my dog need? It is the question we hear most, and the honest answer is uncomfortable: probably more than you are giving, and a different kind than you think.

There is no single number that fits every dog. A twelve-year-old beagle and a two-year-old Malinois do not live in the same world. But most owners land on the low side — not because they do not care, but because a tired-looking dog and a truly conditioned dog look the same from the couch.

Here is how we actually think about it.

## Start with drive, not a breed chart

Forget the generic "thirty minutes a day" advice for a second. What matters is your dog's drive — the internal engine that decides how much output they need to feel settled.

A low-drive dog is content with a sniff-heavy stroll and a nap. A moderate-drive dog wants a real walk plus some play. A high-drive dog — the kind that clears the fence for a squirrel or still has one more gear after a long beach day — needs sustained, structured work, daily, or the energy comes out sideways.

You know which one you have. You live with them.

## A walk is the floor, not the ceiling

A neighborhood walk is good for your dog. It is enrichment, it is sniffing, it is decompression. It is also stop-start, slow, and almost never long enough to drain a dog that was built to work.

Think of it as the floor. For a low-drive dog, the floor might be the whole house. For everyone else, it is the starting line.

## How to read an under-exercised dog

Dogs do not file complaints. They show you. The signs are consistent: pacing in the evening, destruction when left alone, frantic reactions to the doorbell or the leash, trouble settling even when the house is quiet, weight creeping up despite "normal" food.

None of that is a behavior problem first. It is usually an output problem wearing a behavior problem's clothes.

## What "enough" actually looks like

Enough is when the nervous system has nothing left to spend on micro-meltdowns. The dog comes in, drinks, lies down, and actually stays down. They sleep deeper. They meet the next trigger with a shrug instead of a spin.

For a healthy, high-drive adult, that usually means real exertion most days — not a marathon, but sustained effort where the heart rate climbs and holds. Quality of output beats raw minutes every time. If your dog is very young, very old, or has a medical condition, talk to your vet about the right ceiling — this is about healthy dogs with energy to burn.
